A delivery is an obligation to get one event to one endpoint. Railhook keeps trying it on a fixed schedule, the **retry ladder**, until the endpoint answers `2xx` or the ladder runs out. What it gives up on is kept in **Failed Messages** for a person to decide about.

## Delivery statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PENDING` | Created, or waiting for its next attempt to be due. |
| `PROCESSING` | Claimed by a worker for one attempt. A delivery left here by a worker that died is handed back to `PENDING`. |
| `SUCCESS` | The endpoint answered `2xx`. Nothing more is sent. |
| `FAILED` | Given up on because nobody can make another attempt help: the URL is refused, or the endpoint cannot receive it. |
| `DLQ` | The ladder ran out, the endpoint answered with a status another attempt will not change, or the delivery outlived the age cap. Shown in the dashboard as **Failed Messages**. |

## What earns another attempt

| Result of the attempt | What happens |
|---|---|
| `2xx` | `SUCCESS`. |
| `408`, `429`, any `5xx` | Retried on the ladder. This set is the default; a subscription or an incoming destination can change it — see [Which statuses are worth retrying](#which-statuses-are-worth-retrying). |
| A connection error, a timeout, or a host that does not resolve | Retried on the ladder. |
| The transformation failed to apply | Retried on the ladder. The raw payload is never sent instead. |
| Any other `4xx`, or a `3xx` | `DLQ` on the first attempt, without the rest of the ladder. A `401`, a `404` or a redirect will not change on its own, but you can change it: fix the credentials or the URL, then retry from Failed Messages. Redirects are not followed. |
| The endpoint cannot receive it, for example it is not verified, or its URL is refused by the private address check | `FAILED` without an attempt. |

## The retry ladders

Each wait is jittered between 50% and 150% of its value, so a burst of retries does not arrive at once.

| Direction | Attempts | Waits between attempts | Nominal total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outgoing (a delivery) | 7 | 1 min, 5 min, 15 min, 1 h, 6 h, 24 h | about 31 h 21 min |
| Incoming (a forward) | 5 | 1 min, 5 min, 15 min, 1 h | about 1 h 21 min |

These are the defaults. A subscription can set its own `maxAttempts` (1 to 20) and `retryDelays` in seconds, and an incoming destination can set its own too. When there are more attempts than delays, the last delay repeats.

<Aside title="Why the two ladders differ">
On purpose. Outgoing carries your own event to an endpoint you registered, where holding it for a day is a reasonable promise. Incoming relays somebody else's webhook onward, where giving up sooner is the better promise.
</Aside>

### What is not an attempt

Some work is turned away before any request is made: a tenant or per-endpoint rate limit, all concurrency slots in use, an open circuit breaker, or an ordered delivery waiting for an earlier one. That is a **deferral**. It does not use up an attempt or move the ladder; the delivery is simply scheduled again. Only requests that were actually made count.

## Timeouts

One attempt waits 30 seconds for a response unless the subscription sets `timeoutSeconds`, which must be between 1 and 60. A timeout counts as a failed attempt and is retried like any other failure.

## Which statuses are worth retrying

By default a delivery is retried on `408`, `429` and every `5xx`, and nothing else. A subscription can say otherwise with `retryableStatuses`, and an incoming destination has the same field.

The value is a comma-separated list of terms:

| Term | Matches |
|---|---|
| `429` | Exactly that status. |
| `500-599` | An inclusive range. |
| `5xx` | Shorthand for `500-599`. Also `4xx`, `3xx`, `2xx`, `1xx`. |
| `>=500`, `>500`, `<=408`, `<400` | Open at one end. |
| `!501` | Excluded. Any term can be prefixed with `!`. |

Exclusions win wherever they are written, so `5xx,!501` and `!501,5xx` mean the same thing. A few worth knowing:

```text title="Examples"
408,429,500-599    the default — what Railhook did before this was configurable
5xx,!501           retry every server error except "not implemented"
>=500,408,429,409  a receiver that answers 409 while it is still catching up
408,429            a receiver whose 5xx means the payload was rejected for good
```

The API rejects a list it cannot read, with a message naming the term at fault. A status the list does not cover is treated the way a `4xx` is: the delivery goes to Failed Messages on the first attempt, without the rest of the ladder, and you can retry it from there.

<Aside title="A subscription's list is copied onto its deliveries">
Like the ladder, it is stamped on each delivery as that delivery is created. Editing the subscription changes what happens to new deliveries, not to ones already partway through their ladder — a delivery is judged by the rules it started under.
</Aside>

## Retry-After

A receiver answering `429` or `503` can say when to come back, with a `Retry-After` header. Railhook honours it in both forms the HTTP spec allows: a number of seconds, or an HTTP date.

Three rules bound it:

- **Only on `429` and `503`.** Those are the two statuses where the header means "I am over capacity, wait". On a `500` it is at best a guess and at worst a header a proxy left on.
- **It can only push an attempt later.** A receiver asking to be called back sooner than the ladder says is asking Railhook to retry harder than its owner configured, which is not the receiver's decision.
- **It is clamped**, by `WEBHOOK_RETRY_AFTER_MAX_SECONDS` (default `21600`, six hours). One line of a misconfigured response would otherwise park a delivery for a year.

Six hours is the largest tier both default ladders share, so honouring the header never holds a delivery longer than the ladder itself already can, and seven attempts at the maximum still fit inside the 96-hour age cap. Hookdeck allows seven days; Railhook does not, because a week-long wait would be cut short by that cap rather than honoured. Set the variable to `0` to ignore the header entirely.

An honoured `Retry-After` does not buy an extra attempt. A `429` on the last rung goes to Failed Messages like any other failure, however politely it asked.

## The age cap

A delivery still outstanding 96 hours after it was created is moved to Failed Messages whatever its attempt count. A delivery you retried or resent by hand is measured from that moment instead, so an old delivery gets its new attempts. For incoming forwards the cap is 24 hours. The cap catches obligations that stopped moving; the ladder ends the ones that are failing.

| Variable | Default | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `DELIVERY_ESCALATION_HARD_CAP_HOURS` | `96` | Outgoing |
| `FORWARD_ESCALATION_HARD_CAP_HOURS` | `24` | Incoming |

The worker refuses to start if a default ladder, at the top of its jitter, would not fit inside its cap.

## Failed Messages

Failed Messages lists what Railhook abandoned: the endpoint, the event type, how many attempts were made and the last error. The same list is available from the API at `GET /api/v1/projects/{projectId}/dlq`.

**Retrying from Failed Messages** puts the same delivery back to `PENDING` and gives it 3 more attempts. It keeps its delivery id, its attempt history and its `Idempotency-Key`, so a receiver that already processed it can recognise the duplicate. The first new attempt is sent straight away. Retry one delivery with `POST /api/v1/projects/{projectId}/dlq/{deliveryId}/retry`, or several with `POST /api/v1/projects/{projectId}/dlq/retry`.

For incoming webhooks the same screen is called **Failed Forwards**. Retrying a failed forward creates a new forward to the same destination that starts again from attempt 1.

<Aside type="tip" title="Retry or replay?">
A retry is the next attempt on the same delivery. A replay builds new deliveries from events already stored, and leaves the originals as they are. The dashboard calls replay the Time Machine. See [Replay and Time Machine](/docs/outgoing/replay/).
</Aside>
