Railhook retries a delivery on a fixed schedule, the **retry ladder**, until the endpoint answers `2xx` or the ladder runs out. What it gives up on goes to **Failed Messages**.

## Delivery statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PENDING` | Created, or waiting for its next attempt to be due. |
| `PROCESSING` | Claimed by a worker for one attempt. A delivery left here by a worker that died is handed back to `PENDING`. |
| `SUCCESS` | The endpoint answered `2xx`. Nothing more is sent. |
| `FAILED` | Given up on because nobody can make another attempt help: the URL is refused, or the endpoint cannot receive it. |
| `DLQ` | The ladder ran out, the endpoint answered with a status another attempt will not change, or the delivery outlived the age cap. Shown in the dashboard as **Failed Messages**. |

## What earns another attempt

| Result of the attempt | What happens |
|---|---|
| `2xx` | `SUCCESS`. |
| `408`, `429`, any `5xx` | Retried on the ladder. This is the default set. A subscription or an incoming destination can change it: see [Which statuses are worth retrying](#which-statuses-are-worth-retrying). |
| A connection error, a timeout, or a host that does not resolve | Retried on the ladder. |
| The transformation failed to apply | Retried on the ladder. The raw payload is never sent instead. |
| Any other `4xx`, or a `3xx` | `DLQ` on the first attempt, without the rest of the ladder. Fix the credentials or the URL, then retry from Failed Messages. Redirects are not followed. |
| The endpoint cannot receive it, for example it is not verified, or its URL is refused by the private address check | `FAILED` without an attempt. |

## The retry ladders

Each wait is jittered between 50% and 150% of its value, so retries do not arrive in a burst.

| Direction | Attempts | Waits between attempts | Nominal total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outgoing (a delivery) | 7 | 1 min, 5 min, 15 min, 1 h, 6 h, 24 h | about 31 h 21 min |
| Incoming (a forward) | 5 | 1 min, 5 min, 15 min, 1 h | about 1 h 21 min |

A subscription or an incoming destination can set its own `maxAttempts` (1 to 20) and `retryDelays` in seconds. When there are more attempts than delays, the last delay repeats.

<Aside title="Why the two ladders differ">
On purpose. Outgoing carries your own event to an endpoint you registered, so trying for a day is reasonable. Incoming relays someone else's webhook, so giving up sooner is better.
</Aside>

### What is not an attempt

A tenant or per-endpoint rate limit, full concurrency slots, an open circuit breaker, or an ordered delivery waiting for an earlier one stops the request before it is made. That is a **deferral**: it does not use an attempt or move the ladder, the delivery is just scheduled again.

## Timeouts

One attempt waits 30 seconds for a response unless the subscription sets `timeoutSeconds`, which must be between 1 and 60. A timeout is a failed attempt.

## Which statuses are worth retrying

By default a delivery is retried on `408`, `429` and every `5xx`, and nothing else. A subscription or an incoming destination can change this with `retryableStatuses`, a comma-separated list of terms:

| Term | Matches |
|---|---|
| `429` | Exactly that status. |
| `500-599` | An inclusive range. |
| `5xx` | Shorthand for `500-599`. Also `4xx`, `3xx`, `2xx`, `1xx`. |
| `>=500`, `>500`, `<=408`, `<400` | Open at one end. |
| `!501` | Excluded. Any term can be prefixed with `!`. |

Exclusions win wherever they are written: `5xx,!501` and `!501,5xx` mean the same.

```text title="Examples"
408,429,500-599    the default
5xx,!501           retry every server error except "not implemented"
>=500,408,429,409  a receiver that answers 409 while it is still catching up
408,429            a receiver whose 5xx means the payload was rejected for good
```

The API rejects a list it cannot read and names the bad term. A status the list does not cover is treated like a `4xx`: the delivery goes to Failed Messages on the first attempt.

<Aside title="A subscription's list is copied onto its deliveries">
Like the ladder, it is stamped on each delivery when the delivery is created. Editing the subscription affects new deliveries only, not ones already partway through their ladder.
</Aside>

## Retry-After

A receiver answering `429` or `503` can set `Retry-After`. Railhook accepts both HTTP forms: seconds or an HTTP date. Three rules bound it:

- **Only on `429` and `503`.** Only there does it mean "over capacity, wait". On a `500` it is often a header a proxy left on.
- **It can only push an attempt later.** A receiver cannot make Railhook retry harder than the subscription owner configured.
- **It is clamped**, by `WEBHOOK_RETRY_AFTER_MAX_SECONDS` (default `21600`, six hours). Otherwise one misconfigured response could park a delivery for a year.

Six hours is the largest step both default ladders share, and seven attempts at that maximum still fit inside the 96-hour age cap. A longer wait (Hookdeck allows seven days) would be cut short by the cap anyway. Set the variable to `0` to ignore the header.

An honoured `Retry-After` does not add an attempt. A `429` on the last attempt goes to Failed Messages like any other failure.

## The age cap

A delivery still outstanding 96 hours after it was created is moved to Failed Messages whatever its attempt count. A delivery retried or resent by hand is measured from that moment, so it gets its new attempts. For incoming forwards the cap is 24 hours. The cap catches deliveries that stopped moving; the ladder ends the ones that keep failing.

| Variable | Default | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `DELIVERY_ESCALATION_HARD_CAP_HOURS` | `96` | Outgoing |
| `FORWARD_ESCALATION_HARD_CAP_HOURS` | `24` | Incoming |

The worker refuses to start if a default ladder, at the top of its jitter, would not fit inside its cap.

## Failed Messages

Failed Messages lists each abandoned delivery with its endpoint, event type, attempt count and last error. API: `GET /api/v1/projects/{projectId}/dlq`.

**Retrying from Failed Messages** puts the same delivery back to `PENDING` and gives it 3 more attempts. It keeps its delivery id, attempt history and `Idempotency-Key`, so a receiver that already processed it can detect the duplicate. The first new attempt is sent at once. Retry one delivery with `POST /api/v1/projects/{projectId}/dlq/{deliveryId}/retry`, or several with `POST /api/v1/projects/{projectId}/dlq/retry`.

For incoming webhooks the same screen is called **Failed Forwards**. Retrying one creates a new forward to the same destination, starting from attempt 1.

<Aside type="tip" title="Retry or replay?">
A retry is the next attempt on the same delivery. A replay builds new deliveries from stored events and leaves the originals alone (the dashboard's Time Machine). See [Replay and Time Machine](/docs/outgoing/replay/).
</Aside>
